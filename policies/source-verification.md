# Source verification policy

Current public facts must be verified against current primary sources before they are used in decisions, analyses, issues, pull requests, or published artifacts.

- Verify official names, dates, numbers, versions, and specifications.
- Remove any factual statement that cannot be confirmed.
- Distinguish event date, publication date, accounting period, forecast period, and observation date.
- Prefer official filings, documentation, standards, release notes, datasets, and first-party announcements.
- Recheck the final result after drafting and remove stale or unsupported content.
- Cite exact URLs or connected-source references at the claim they support.

Repository state must be verified through current GitHub data. Previous conversations and status summaries are context, not proof of current state.
